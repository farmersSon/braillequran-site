## Lesson 11: Important Notes

1.  Wasl: continuing, proceeding with reading; Waqf: stopping at the end of a word. When stopping (Waqf), all vowels and Tanwin are dropped and become Sukun. Fatha Tanwin is pronounced with Alif elongation. Example:
    [عالَمينَ] [أَحَدٌ] [فَلَقِ]
    [أَفْواجاً] [تَوّاباً] [أَبَداً]
    [جَمْعاً] [لِباساً]

2.  An Alif is placed after every letter with Fatha Tanwin. Only Ta Marbuta and Hamza-Alif are not written with Alif; when stopping, Ta Marbuta becomes a silent Ha; Hamza-Alif is pronounced like an elongation. Example: [قِي مَةً]
    [سَكينَةٌ] [ثَناءً] [بِناءً]
    [هَباءً] [بَرِيَّةُ]\
    If the last letter of a word with Fatha Tanwin is [ل], then
    [لا] is written:
    عَمَلاً رَسولاً شَكْلاً قَوْلاً جِبِلاًّ

3.  In the Quran, under some words [مد] (elongation) and under some [قسر] (shortening) appear as small text. These indicate that the writing and pronunciation differ. Under words written with elongation letters but not pronounced with elongation, [قسر] always appears. For words written with elongation but read short, we have written the vowel before the elongation to indicate short reading.\
    [أَنَا] [أُولِ] [أُولاءِ]
    [أُولٰئِكَ] [لاَتَّخوَ] [لاَتَّبَعْتُمْ]
    [أَفَائِنْ] [مَلاَئِه۪] [ثَمودَا]
    [يَايْءَسُ] [نَدْعُوَا] [لِشَايْءٍ]

4.  In cases where a single Waw or Ya is written and serves as both a letter and an elongation, [مد] appears below; when it should not be elongated, [قصر] appears. Of these, we have marked those with shortening using only the vowel and those with elongation using hidden elongations.\
    [يُبْدِؤ۫نَ] [جاؤُكَ] [نَبِيّ۪نَ]
    [يَسْتَهْزَؤ۫نَ] [داو۫دَ] [يَشاؤُ]
    etc.

5.  The following words are pronounced short when continuing (Wasl) and with elongation when stopping (Waqf):\
    [أَنَا] [لٰكِنَّا] [الظُّنونَا]
    [رَسولاَ] [سَبيلاَ] [قَواريرَا]
    [سَلاسِلاَ]

6.  In Arabic, there are two types of Alif regarding strength: Fixed Alif: It always takes a vowel and is pronounced. Wasl-Alif: (connecting Alif) It is pronounced when vowelized. In certain cases it is unvoweled and not pronounced. In Braille, the Wasl-Alif is written with Fatha without a vowel even in cases where it is pronounced.\
    [الْحَمْدُ] [الرَّحْمٰنُ] [الَّذي]
    [الَّتي] [اُنْظُرْ] [اِقْتَرَبَ]
    etc.

7.  Alif Za'id: After the Waw at the end of some words there is an unpronounced Alif. This is called Alif Za'id; it indicates that the word is a plural verb.\
    [قالوا] [عَمِلوا] [يُؤْمِنوا]
    [يَكونوا] [كُلوا] [يَرَوْا]
    [دَعَوْا] [مَشَوْا] [فَأْو۫ا]
    etc.

8.  The following words are pronounced with elongation both when continuing (Wasl) and when stopping (Waqf):\
    [يُحْي۪] [يَسْتَحْي۪] [وَلِيّ۪] The endings of the following words also transform into an elongation when stopping:\
    [هُوَ] [هِيَ] [فَنَسِيَ] [مَثانِيَ]

9.  Huruf al-Muqatta'a: At the beginning of 29 suras there are verses consisting of one or more letters. In these, instead of a vowel there is a straight line above.\
    This straight line indicates that we pronounce these letters elongated by four Alif measures.\
    [ا - ل - م] [ن] [حٰـم] [ع - س -
    ق] [طٰ - هٰ] [يٰ - س] [ق]
    [ا - ل - رٰ]
    etc.

10. If after an elongation a word beginning with [ا] (Wasl-Alif) follows, the elongation becomes a vowel. [إلى اللّٰهِ] [في
    الدّينِ] [إِلاّ امْرَأَةٍ] [عيسى ابْنِ] [ذو
    انْتِقامٍ] [قالوا ادْعُ]

11. If after a Tanwin a word beginning with Wasl-Alif follows, the Nun of the Tanwin is pronounced with an assumed Kasra. [عاداً
    الْأولى] [صِراطاً الْمُسْتَقيمَ] [مُحَمَّداً
    الْمُصْطَفى] [رَجُلٌ افْتَرى] [لوطٍ الْمُرْسَلينَ]
    [خَيْرٌ اطْمَأَنَّ] etc.
