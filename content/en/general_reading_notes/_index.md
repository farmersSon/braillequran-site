## General Notes


- When reading Braille, even if it is Arabic, *it must always be read from left to right.*

- The Arabic letters are written together with their signs, one after another. Example:
  when [لَ] is written, the Fatha is placed after the letter Lam in the Braille
  alphabet.

- In some paragraphs, words unrelated to the topic have been written in the Braille
  edition of the Elif juz. For example, in the paragraph dealing with the Hamza there
  is an example with Tanwin. However, in terms of ordering, Tanwin is covered after the
  Hamza. To draw attention to this point and at the same time not to disrupt the layout
  in the Braille edition, the word written in Arabic in this case has been marked in
  yellow.

- In Arabic, the shape of a letter changes according to its position in a word. In
  Braille, however, each letter *always* has a single shape.

- The mushafs available in Türkiye differ in a few respects from, for example, the
  mushafs used in Saudi Arabia. The following differences are important in the coming
  lessons:

  1.  The writing of Alif Maqsura instead of Alif [سَوّىهُ]; in the mushafs found in
      Saudi Arabia the same word appears as follows: [سَوّاهُ]

  2.  The use of the Hamza-Alif instead of the Hamza

  In both differences the system currently available in Türkiye is preferred.

- In the Arabic Braille system used in Türkiye, vowels of the same kind are not written
  before the Madd letters. In the mushafs, however, the vowels are present. This
  difference is also covered in the tenth lesson
